### Question States
People use the states, "good" and "bad" to describe expected profit or less from the question. The state we give a question does not affect the answer. A better way of classifying questions is rough vs precise questions
- A rough question would be: How can I hack X
- a precise question would be: How can I sue the server's SMB service to identify its existing user accounts?
with these two examples you can clearly see the affect of the question on the answer you receive. 
### Questions in General
Questions are an integral part of the thinking process. let us consider following a recipe to bake a cake ultimately it all answers one rough question
- How do I bake this cake
the two more precise questions we need to answer are
- what ingredients are needed
- what methods of preparation do we use 
Learning material is the ingredients, the preparation method is our questions. 
the ideal flow here is that we know our goal and are willing to pursue it. we use principles such as the Pareto principle and Occam's Razor to develop our Talents and skills. we also used our decision making to pass the obstacles that obstruct our path by asking the right questions. 

questions are based on three aspects which we build our questions on
1. origin
2. process
3. result/goal

### Relationship-Oriented-Questioning Model
for every question you have
- your current position
- the object of your question
- knowns
- unknowns
- other positions
if you can evaluate your knowns and how they relate to you and the object of your question you can use that to minimize your unknowns and to ask more precise questions to make your unknowns knowns 
If there is no clear answer, this model does not work. 
so ultimately we need to know, what is the right question
- A right question is a precise question that allows us to establish the relationship between the components, understand them, and take us one step further to the required answer.
